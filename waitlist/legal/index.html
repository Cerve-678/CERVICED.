Reviews Policy

How reviews work on Cerviced, and what we do to keep them genuine.

1Who can leave a review

Every review on Cerviced is linked to a booking made through the app, so only clients who booked a treatment can review it.

2What a review must be

A review must be honest, about your own experience of the treatment and the service, and written by you. Do not include anyone's personal information, and do not post anything abusive, discriminatory or unlawful.

3What is not allowed

  • Reviewing a business you own, work for, are related to or compete with.
  • Offering, asking for or accepting anything in return for a review, such as a discount or a free treatment.
  • Writing, buying or selling fake reviews, or asking someone else to.
  • Providers only asking happy clients to leave a review.
  • Providers pressuring a client to change or remove a review.

4How reviews are shown

Reviews appear on the provider's profile. A provider's rating is the average of their reviews' star ratings, and rating is one of the things Search uses to order results, as section 6 of our Terms explains.

5How we moderate reviews

We do not check every review before it appears. We remove or hide reviews that break this policy, and we may hide a review for a short time while we look into a report. We do not remove a review just because it is negative or because a provider disagrees with it. If a review reveals someone's personal or health information, we will remove that information.

6Reporting a review

If you think a review breaks this policy, email support@cerviced.co with the provider's name, the review, and why you are reporting it.

7Breaches of this policy

We may remove the reviews involved, warn the people responsible, and suspend or close accounts that break this policy more than once or seriously.

Health & Safety

What Cerviced does and does not do to keep treatments safe, and what you can do to look after yourself.

1What Cerviced checks

Providers run their own businesses and are responsible for the treatments they give. Cerviced does not check providers' identity, qualifications, licences, insurance or DBS checks. Anything a provider tells us about them is self-declared, and we never present it as checked.

Before you book, ask the provider about their training, qualifications and insurance, and about any licence the treatment needs.

2Your health

  • Tell your provider about allergies, pregnancy, medication, skin conditions and anything else that could affect your treatment, even if they do not ask.
  • Some services show whether the provider considers them suitable during pregnancy, whether a patch test is needed, and reasons a treatment may not be suitable for you. This information comes from the provider, not from Cerviced.
  • Follow your provider's patch-test and aftercare instructions. A provider may refuse or stop a treatment if they think it is not safe for you.
  • Cerviced does not give medical advice. If you are unsure whether a treatment is right for you, speak to a doctor or pharmacist before booking.

3Injectables and advanced treatments

Anti-wrinkle injections (Botox) are a prescription-only medicine. Before treatment, you should have a face-to-face consultation with a qualified prescriber. Ask who the prescriber is, what is being used, and who to contact if you have a problem afterwards.

If you have a reaction after any treatment, contact the provider and get medical advice: call NHS 111, or 999 in an emergency. You can report side effects of medicines and problems with cosmetic products through the MHRA Yellow Card scheme at yellowcard.mhra.gov.uk.

4Age limits

You must be 16 or over to have a Cerviced account. Some treatments have higher legal minimum ages. In England, Botox and dermal fillers cannot be given to anyone under 18 for cosmetic reasons, and tattooing, including semi-permanent makeup and microblading, is illegal for under-18s. Other treatments may need a parent's consent, and providers can refuse a booking that breaks these rules.

5Home visits

  • Your address is only shared with the provider once they have accepted your booking.
  • You may have another person present during your appointment.
  • Either of you can stop the appointment if you feel unsafe.
  • For a booking made through Cerviced, pay in the app. Be cautious of any request to pay into a personal bank account instead.

6Providers' own booking pages

When you book on a provider's own booking page, it is their website, not ours. Cerviced cannot see what you enter there or help with bookings made there. See section 20 of our Terms.

7Reporting a safety concern

If you are in danger, call 999. If a provider or client has made you feel unsafe, or you think a treatment caused you harm, email support@cerviced.co with your booking reference. We take safety reports seriously and may pause a profile while we look into it. If you think a crime has been committed, report it to the police on 101, or 999 in an emergency.

Complaints & Reporting

How to raise a problem with a treatment, with Cerviced, or with something you have seen on Cerviced, and what happens next.

1A problem with a treatment

Tell the provider first, as they are best placed to put it right, for example by redoing the treatment. If you cannot resolve it with them, open the booking in the app to request a refund (until the end of the fifth day after the day of your appointment) or make a complaint, or email support@cerviced.co with your booking reference. We will do our best to help, but the provider remains responsible for the service they gave you.

2A complaint about Cerviced

Email support@cerviced.co with what happened, any booking reference, and what you would like us to do. We will acknowledge your complaint within 5 working days and aim to give you a full response within 4 weeks. If we need longer, we will tell you why and when to expect it. Providers can use the same address for complaints about how Cerviced treats their business.

3Reporting content or an account

To report content, a profile, a review, a message or illegal content, email support@cerviced.co and tell us what it is, where it is, and why you are reporting it. We review reports promptly, prioritising safety and illegal content, and we will tell you what we have done where we can. See our Community Guidelines.

4Appealing a decision

If we remove your content, or limit, suspend or close your account, and you think we got it wrong, reply to our message or email us with why. We will look at the decision again and tell you the outcome. If we were wrong, we will put things back.

5If a complaint cannot be resolved

We will explain our final position in writing. If you are still unhappy, you can get free advice from the Citizens Advice consumer helpline on 0808 223 1133 (or Advice Direct Scotland on 0808 164 6000 in Scotland), or take your claim to court. Where an approved alternative dispute resolution provider could help, we will give you its details and tell you whether we agree to use it.

6Privacy complaints

If your complaint is about how we have handled your personal information, you can also complain to the Information Commissioner's Office at ico.org.uk or on 0303 123 1113.

7Payment disputes

You can contact your card provider about a payment at any time. If you raise a chargeback, please also let us know so that we can help resolve it.
