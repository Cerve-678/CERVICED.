Cerviced accepts payments for bookings as the provider's commercial agent. When you pay for a booking in the app, your payment to Cerviced counts as payment to the provider for that amount.

13Cancellations by You

15Refunds and Refund Requests

Each fee is fixed at the amount shown when you book, can be collected only once per booking, and can never be more than half the price of the booking. You will be notified whenever a fee is charged or retried. If you believe a fee is wrong, you can dispute it with the provider in the app within 7 days of the charge, and the provider decides whether to refund it. Fees are paid to the provider, not to Cerviced, and Cerviced does not decide fee disputes. This does not affect your statutory rights, including your right to contact your bank.

Fees, Cancellations & Refunds

How paying, cancelling, rescheduling and refunds work for bookings you pay for in the Cerviced app. The legal wording is in sections 9 to 16 of our Terms & Conditions. This policy explains it in more detail.

In short

  • Providers set their prices. You also pay a Cerviced service fee, shown with your total before you pay.
  • Each provider has their own cancellation policy. It's shown before you book and applied automatically if you cancel.
  • If the provider declines or cancels, you get everything back, including the service fee.
  • If you only get part of your money back, the service fee isn't refunded.
  • The remaining balance after a deposit is paid to the provider in person. Cerviced isn't involved in it.

1What you pay

Providers set their own prices, in pounds sterling. When you book in the app, a provider may ask you to pay the full price or a deposit, or let you choose. A deposit can be a percentage of the price or a fixed amount. Your total, including any deposit and Cerviced's service fee, is shown before you pay.

Payments are processed by Stripe. Depending on your device, you can pay by card, Apple Pay, Google Pay or PayPal.

2Cerviced's service fee

Cerviced charges clients one service fee per checkout. It's added on top of the provider's prices and never comes out of what the provider receives. The fee depends on the value of the treatments you're booking. If you're only paying deposits, it's based on the full price of the treatments, not the deposit amount. You'll always see the exact fee, and your total, before you pay.

The service fee is refunded if your booking is refunded in full, for example because the provider declined or cancelled it, or because you cancelled before their notice period. It isn't refunded if you get a partial refund.

3Deposits and remaining balances

If you pay a deposit, you pay the rest of the price to the provider at your appointment, in whatever way they accept. Cerviced doesn't collect, hold, check or guarantee that payment, and can't settle disagreements about whether or how much was paid.

4Cancelling a booking

You can cancel from the booking in the app. Each provider sets a notice period and what they keep if you cancel inside it. Both are shown on their profile and before you book.

  • You cancel before the notice period: you get a full refund, including the service fee.
  • You cancel inside the notice period: the provider keeps the share their policy states, and the rest is refunded to you. The service fee isn't refunded.

Late cancellations and missed appointments are recorded, and the provider you booked with can see how often this has happened with them.

5If the provider declines or cancels

You're refunded in full automatically, including the service fee. The same happens if a provider doesn't respond to your booking request before it expires.

6Rescheduling

You can ask to move a booking from the booking itself. The provider can accept your new time or suggest another, and providers can also suggest a new time to you. A request that isn't answered in time expires, and your original booking stays as it was. Providers may limit how close to the appointment you can reschedule. This is shown in their policy.

7Missed appointments

If you don't attend an appointment, you won't get a refund for what you paid unless the provider chooses to give one. If you agreed to a no-show fee when you booked, the provider may also charge it (see section 8).

8Late arrival and no-show fees

This is the wording you agree to at checkout when a provider sets one of these fees.

Some providers set a late arrival fee, a no-show fee, or both. Any fee is shown to you before you pay, and you must agree to it at checkout to book. If you agree, your card is saved securely by Stripe, and the provider may charge the agreed fee to it within 24 hours of your appointment: the late arrival fee if you arrive after the provider's stated grace period, or the no-show fee if you do not attend. If the charge is declined, we will ask you to pay the fee in the app or by email within 7 days, and we may try your saved card again up to three more times in those 7 days. If your bank asks you to approve the charge, we won't try the card again; you can pay it yourself within the 7 days. Each fee is fixed at the amount shown when you book, can be collected only once per booking, and can never be more than half the price of the booking. You will be notified whenever a fee is charged or retried. If you believe a fee is wrong, you can dispute it with the provider in the app within 7 days of the charge, and the provider decides whether to refund it. Fees are paid to the provider, not to Cerviced, and Cerviced does not decide fee disputes. This does not affect your statutory rights, including your right to contact your bank.

9Asking for a refund

If something went wrong, you can request a refund from the booking in the app and tell us why. Your request goes to the provider first. They can refund you in full or in part, or pass the request to Cerviced. If it's passed to us, we'll look at what happened, including the provider's policy, and tell you the outcome.

Cerviced only reviews money paid through the app. Payments you made to the provider in person, or on their own booking page, are between you and them.

Refunds go back to the card or account you paid with and usually take 5 to 10 working days to appear, depending on your bank.

10Bookings made on a provider's own booking page

Some providers take bookings on their own booking page instead of through Cerviced. Payments, cancellations and refunds for those bookings are handled by the provider under their own terms. Cerviced can't change, cancel or refund them.

11Keep Cerviced bookings in the app

What Cerviced does and doesn't do to keep treatments safe, and what you can do to look after yourself.

In short

  • Providers are independent. Cerviced doesn't check their qualifications, licences, insurance or DBS checks.
  • Tell your provider about allergies, pregnancy and health conditions before your treatment.
  • Some treatments have legal age limits, including Botox, fillers and semi-permanent makeup.
  • In an emergency, call 999. For urgent medical advice, call NHS 111.

1What Cerviced checks

Providers run their own businesses and are responsible for the treatments they give. Cerviced doesn't check providers' identity, qualifications, licences, insurance or DBS checks. Anything a provider tells us about them is self-declared, and we never present it as checked.

Before you book, ask the provider about their training, qualifications and insurance, and about any licence the treatment needs. A good provider will be happy to tell you.

2Your health

  • Tell your provider about allergies, pregnancy, medication, skin conditions and anything else that could affect your treatment, even if they don't ask.
  • Some services show whether the provider considers them suitable during pregnancy, whether a patch test is needed, and reasons a treatment may not be suitable for you. This information comes from the provider, not from Cerviced.
  • Follow your provider's patch-test and aftercare instructions. A provider may refuse or stop a treatment if they think it isn't safe for you.
  • Cerviced doesn't give medical advice. If you're unsure whether a treatment is right for you, speak to a doctor or pharmacist before booking.

3Injectables and advanced treatments

Anti-wrinkle injections (Botox) are a prescription-only medicine. Before treatment, you should have a face-to-face consultation with a qualified prescriber. Ask who the prescriber is, what's being used, and who to contact if you have a problem afterwards.

If you have a reaction after any treatment, contact the provider and get medical advice: call NHS 111, or 999 in an emergency. You can report side effects of medicines and problems with cosmetic products through the MHRA Yellow Card scheme at yellowcard.mhra.gov.uk.

4Age limits

You must be 16 or over to have a Cerviced account. Some treatments have higher legal minimum ages. In England, Botox and dermal fillers can't be given to anyone under 18 for cosmetic reasons, and tattooing, including semi-permanent makeup and microblading, is illegal for under-18s. Other treatments may need a parent's consent, and providers can refuse a booking that breaks these rules.

5Home visits

  • Your address is only shared with the provider once they've accepted your booking.
  • You're welcome to have someone else at home during your appointment.
  • Either of you can stop the appointment if you feel unsafe.
  • For a booking made through Cerviced, pay in the app. Be wary of any request to pay into a personal bank account instead.

6Providers' own booking pages

When you book on a provider's own booking page, it's their website, not ours. Cerviced can't see what you enter there or help with bookings made there. See section 20 of our Terms.

7Reporting a safety concern

If you're in danger, call 999. If a provider or client has made you feel unsafe, or you think a treatment caused you harm, email support@cerviced.co with your booking reference. We take safety reports seriously and may pause a profile while we look into it. If you think a crime has been committed, report it to the police on 101, or 999 in an emergency.

Complaints & Reporting

How to raise a problem with a treatment, with Cerviced, or with something you've seen on Cerviced, and what happens next.

In short

  • Problem with a treatment: speak to the provider first, then to us.
  • Problem with Cerviced, or something to report: email support@cerviced.co.
  • We'll acknowledge your complaint within 5 working days and aim to reply in full within 4 weeks.

1A problem with a treatment

Tell the provider first, as they're best placed to put it right, for example by redoing the treatment. If you can't sort it out with them, open the booking in the app to request a refund or make a complaint, or email support@cerviced.co with your booking reference. We'll try to help, but the provider remains responsible for the service they gave you.

2A complaint about Cerviced

Email support@cerviced.co with what happened, any booking reference, and what you'd like us to do. We'll acknowledge your complaint within 5 working days and aim to give you a full response within 4 weeks. If we need longer, we'll tell you why and when to expect it. Providers can use the same address for complaints about how Cerviced treats their business.

3Reporting content or an account

To report content, a profile, a review, a message or illegal content, email support@cerviced.co and tell us what it is, where it is, and why you're reporting it. We review reports promptly, prioritising safety and illegal content, and we'll tell you what we've done where we can. See our Community Guidelines.

4Appealing a decision

If we remove your content, or limit, suspend or close your account, and you think we got it wrong, reply to our message or email us with why. We'll look at the decision again and tell you the outcome. If we were wrong, we'll put things back.

5If we can't resolve it

We'll explain our final position in writing. If you're still unhappy, you can get free advice from the Citizens Advice consumer helpline on 0808 223 1133 (or Advice Direct Scotland on 0808 164 6000 in Scotland), or take your claim to court. Where an approved alternative dispute resolution provider could help, we'll give you its details and tell you whether we agree to use it.

6Privacy complaints

If your complaint is about how we've handled your personal information, you can also complain to the Information Commissioner's Office at ico.org.uk or on 0303 123 1113.

7Payment disputes

You can contact your card provider about a payment at any time. If you raise a chargeback, please let us know too, so we can help resolve it.
